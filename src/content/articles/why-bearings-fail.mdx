<p class="lead">A rolling bearing is designed to die of one thing only: metal fatigue after millions of revolutions. That is what its L10 rating predicts. In real plants, most bearings are pulled long before that point. Something else got them first.</p>

## The four causes

A widely cited breakdown from SKF splits early bearing failures into four groups. The exact numbers vary by industry, but the order rarely changes.

| Cause | Share of failures | What you see |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Poor lubrication** | 36% | Discoloured races, glazing, smearing, burnt grease |
| **Fatigue** | 34% | Flaking (spalling) in the load zone |
| **Poor fitting** | 16% | Brinelling dents, cracked rings, fretting rust |
| **Contamination** | 14% | Pitting, scratches, abrasive wear bands |

## Lubrication: too little, too much, or the wrong one

The oil film between ball and race is thinner than a human hair. When it breaks down, metal touches metal and the surfaces heat, smear and wear. The causes are usually ordinary: a missed PM, the wrong grease, two incompatible greases mixed, or more grease than the housing can take.

Overgreasing is as dangerous as starving the bearing. A housing packed full churns the grease, runs hot and pushes the seals out. Heat then cooks the grease and you are back to no lubrication.

## Fatigue: the only natural death

Every time a ball rolls over a point on the race, the steel just below the surface is stressed. After enough cycles, small cracks form under the surface and grow until a flake of metal breaks out. That is spalling. A spall starts small and spreads quickly, so vibration rises fast once it begins.

If fatigue shows up early, the bearing was usually overloaded: misalignment, a belt tensioned too tight, an unbalanced rotor, or a bearing undersized for the job.

## Poor fitting: damage done on day one

Many bearings are damaged during installation. Driving a bearing on with a hammer through the rolling elements leaves evenly spaced dents in the races at the ball pitch. That is brinelling, and the bearing is noisy from the first start.

- Press or heat the ring that has the interference fit, never the other one.
- Use an induction heater, not a torch. Keep the bearing below about 120 °C.
- Check shaft and housing dimensions. A loose fit lets the ring creep and fret.

<Danger stamp="DON'T">
**Never spin a bearing with compressed air.** The rolling elements can overspeed and the bearing can come apart.
</Danger>

## Contamination: dirt and water get in

Hard particles get rolled into the races and leave dents. Each dent becomes a stress point and an early start for fatigue. Water is just as harmful: it breaks down the oil film and rusts the surfaces.

Clean the fitting before you grease it, cap open bearings during repairs, and keep spares in their packaging until the moment you install them.

## Read the failed bearing

A failed bearing is evidence. Before it goes in the scrap bin, look at the races and note where the damage sits: in the load zone, all the way around, or off to one side. A wear path running on a diagonal usually points to misalignment. Attach the photo to the work order so the next person sees the pattern.

<Takeaway>
Replace the bearing without fixing the cause and you will be replacing it again.
</Takeaway>
