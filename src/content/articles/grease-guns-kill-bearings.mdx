<p class="lead">Ask five technicians how many pumps of grease go into a motor bearing and you will get five answers. That is the problem. Greasing by feel leads to two failures: starved bearings on machines nobody remembers, and packed bearings on machines everybody loves.</p>

## Know your gun

Grease guns deliver anywhere from about 0.9 to 1.8 grams per stroke depending on the model. Weigh ten strokes on a scale and write the result on the gun. Now "3 pumps" means something.

## Don't mix greases

Greases with different thickeners can react when mixed. The blend can soften and run out, or harden. Lithium complex and polyurea, for example, are often listed as incompatible. Label every grease point with the grease it takes, and color-code the guns.

## Open the drain plug

On motors with a drain plug, remove it before greasing and run the motor for a while after. Old grease has a way out, and the housing does not build pressure that pushes grease past the seal into the windings.

<Danger>
Follow your site's lockout and machine-guarding rules when greasing near moving parts. If a point can't be reached safely while running, extend it with a remote grease line.
</Danger>

## Use your ears

An ultrasonic detector lets you hear the bearing while greasing. When the noise drops and then starts to rise again, stop. It turns guesswork into a measurement for a modest cost.
